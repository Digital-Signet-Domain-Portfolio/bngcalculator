BNG S106 legal cost: Section 106 vs conservation covenant

A BNG s106 carries two costs: legal fees for the agreement, and a one-off monitoring contribution the council publishes: Cotswold £4,290 to £18,050 (April 2026), East Hampshire £10,986 to £25,681 (from 1 April 2026). For a habitat bank, Mid Sussex charges a £7,000 set-up fee covering its review and the drafting of the legal agreement, plus £18,000 for 30 years of monitoring (2026/27).

Legal fees

Two sets of legal costs arise: your own solicitor’s for drafting, negotiating and executing the deed, and the planning authority’s, which councils recharge to the applicant. Cotswold and Mid Sussex both say legal fees are levied on top of their BNG monitoring fees, without publishing a figure.[S20] The nearest published figure is for habitat banks in Mid Sussex: its £7,000 set-up fee covers the time it spends reviewing the proposal and drafting the legal agreement, alongside a £1,000 year 0 inspection fee and an £18,000 30-year monitoring fee (2026/27).[S25] Ask both sides for a fixed fee before drafting starts.

Council monitoring contribution

On top of legal fees, councils charge a one-off contribution to monitor the habitat for 30 years. Cotswold District Council charges £4,290 to £18,050 for sites up to 40 ha (April 2026), scaled by area and creation difficulty, and says a separate fee covers the legal costs of preparing the s106.[S20] East Hampshire charges £10,986 to £25,681 by site area from 1 April 2026.[S21] See the 30-year management cost page for the full bands.

S106 versus conservation covenant

The conservation covenant is an instrument under the Environment Act 2021 that attaches binding obligations to land. It can only be made with a designated responsible body. GOV.UK lists 48 (updated 14 May 2026), among them Natural England, the Environment Agency, The Environment Bank Limited, Kent Wildlife Trust, Plantlife and several councils; the National Trust and RSPB are not on it.[S7] Responsible bodies set their own fees.

When the authority reviews the deed

Timing varies by authority: some review the s106 at drafting, others at the post-decision discharge stage. Cotswold says its monitoring contribution is normally payable before the habitat works start.[S20]
